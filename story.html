The Green Frog Inn is a 1933 neighborhood sports pub on the corner of Spring & Sherman in Fort Wayne — built on the simple idea that good drinks, hot food, and a place where everyone knows your name are worth keeping the lights on for. Almost a century later, we're still at it.

why a green frog?

Honestly nobody quite remembers. The story goes there used to be a swampy patch out back of the building when the place opened in '33 — full of tree frogs come summer. Someone painted one on the door for luck and the name stuck for 90 years.

Today it just feels right. We're stubborn, we're loud at the right times, and the regulars never leave. The frog is on the door. The frog is on the menu. The frog is on every glass. Welcome to the inn.

"come for the drinks. stay for the company."

we pour

drafts worth pulling up a stool for.

Our house tap is $2.25 the way it should be. Our guest taps rotate weekly — local pours from across northeast Indiana, a couple of national craft names you'll recognize, and at least one weird one we picked up because we liked the label. Ask the bartender. They have opinions.

we fry

frog legs the regulars won't let us lose.

The first time we tried to quietly retire the frog legs in 2018, three regulars threatened to walk out. They came back the next day. So did the frog legs. They're battered to order, fried golden, served with slaw and pub fries and a side of horsey sauce because that's how they've always been served.

tip: thursday is all-you-can-eat frog leg night. you've been warned.

we grind

nine burgers off the same flat top.

Every burger is a 1/3-lb hand-formed steakburger smashed on the same seasoned flat-top we've used for the last decade. The classic is $5.99 and we'll never raise it. The cardiac arrest will hurt you. The frog burger is the move. Tuesday is build-your-own night — $2 off any burger.

why bloomingdale

a love letter to the neighborhood.

The Frog has been a Bloomingdale fixture since 1933 — through prohibition's end, three wars, twelve presidents, and one pandemic. Cindy Henry — wife of late Mayor Tom Henry — ran the place for decades. She passed in early 2024. Eric Kissinger bought the Frog that October and we re-opened the doors to the same neighborhood that's kept us alive for ninety years.
